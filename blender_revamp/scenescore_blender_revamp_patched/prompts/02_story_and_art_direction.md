# 02: create a meaningful, restrained visual brief

Depends on 00. Own the agreed brief/reference scope. Avoid editing shared scene implementation while the tooling/test workers run.

Develop two compact concepts, choose one based on causal clarity and implementation cost, and proceed without requesting approval of routine aesthetic choices. A minimal character throwing a ball at stacked blocks in a small practice court is a good starting concept, not a mandatory asset list. A visible mechanical launcher is a valid alternative if it produces a more coherent result. A decorative stick figure that never holds or releases the ball fails the purpose.

For the chosen concept specify:

- A one-sentence action and readable beats: establish intention/support, anticipation, release, travel, contact or miss, response, settling and an ending with consequence. Make the same scene understandable without sound or labels.
- Spatial logic: where the actor/launcher stands, what supports the target, the travel lane, the floor and the final resting region. Only include setting elements that establish scale, purpose or depth.
- A restrained palette, understandable material families, edge treatment, contact lighting, camera/framing policy and scene scale. Make the action occupy enough of the frame to read at preview resolution. No debug object labels in the final beauty pass. Avoid ornamental gradients, excessive props, bloom, blur and camera movement that hides failures.
- A matched near-miss concept that changes launch/aim enough to miss but retains the setting, materials, identities and physics. The target remains stable without a genuine disturbance. The miss remains visually distinguishable at delivery size.

Use `references/POSITIVE_NEGATIVE_EXAMPLES.md` to write concrete accept/reject pairs for contact visibility, character/ball attachment, response timing, endings and set design. Optionally consult a few legitimate primary visual references, recording exactly what was viewed and the qualities being adapted. Do not claim a reference video was watched from its title or thumbnail. Avoid copied characters, branded sets or unnecessary external assets.

Favor stylistic simplicity with credible physical behavior. Full photorealism and a complex character rig are optional. A simple figure still needs grounded feet, purposeful posing, a coherent grip/release and follow-through.

Deliver a compact shot/beat plan, material/scale rationale, positive/negative reference table and draft diagnostic camera views. Timing remains provisional until a physical motion test; do not force gravity-driven motion to fit inherited normalized keyframe times.
