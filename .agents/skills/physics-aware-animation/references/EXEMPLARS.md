# Supplied visual references

These notes come from image inspection of extracted frames, not a recovered
`.blend` scene or a physical validation. Exact file hashes and probe fields are
in pack `evidence/reference_metadata.json`. Third-party media is not bundled in
this pack; retain the user's original recordings separately and record reuse
permissions before publishing a shared exemplar library.

## Landscape recording — 11.21.03 AM

41.001667 s, 2138×1182. The file contains one H.264 video stream and no audio stream.
The nominal rate is 60 fps but average rate differs; use actual presentation times.
Twelve overview frames were inspected, plus 20 frames at 0.25 s spacing over
3.5–8.25 s. This is not an exhaustive native-rate motion review.

Visible qualities: silver/white structural surfaces, reflective marbles, bright
yellow mechanism accents, rail-guided paths at several elevations, visible carriers
and circular routes, restrained studio lighting, close views followed by wider
views revealing more of the machine. Around the inspected 4.75–5.75 s interval,
a yellow carrier changes orientation while a marble transfers toward a lower
route. That observation does not identify whether the carrier is motorized,
keyframed or dynamically constrained. Do not invent masses, friction or forces.
Later frames include a bowl with several marbles and end-screen overlays; exclude
those overlays from aesthetic/temporal evaluation windows.

Transfer: layered but legible routing, visible purpose for each mechanism,
strong accent hierarchy, clear contact surfaces and a camera that reveals stages.
Do not inherit all the simultaneous marble activity: the user's sparse,
non-overlapping salient-event constraint remains authoritative.

## Portrait recording — 11.18.05 AM

22.985 s, 762×868. One H.264 video stream, no audio stream. Nominal and average
frame rates differ here too. Twelve overview frames were inspected. A 0–4.75 s
quarter-second contact sheet was also extracted; it is an inspection aid, not
a completed collision annotation or native-motion review.

Visible qualities: blue spiral channels, green/red transitions, a tall support/
elevator structure, multiple differently colored balls and an outdoor setting.
Camera framing changes reveal the route from different positions. The spatial
lesson is a connected route with height, curve and direction changes, not merely
a ball moving over an unstructured flat plane. The image finish differs from the
studio machine; do not force both into one undifferentiated style target.

Transfer: topology of the route, alternation of movement types, visible supports
and sustained travel between events. Do not use the screen recording to infer
exact real-world speed, dimensions, contact timing or collision implementation.

## Use as few-shot material

Each generation run should log the reference clip/interval and the particular
lesson consumed. Pair a style/reference clip with an independently tested
mechanical example; they need not be the same file. A single still is useful for
materials/layout but cannot teach a complete interaction sequence. Review final
motion at native timing before promoting any recreated example to "tested".

Create original implementation examples rather than promising exact pixel-level
reconstruction. The missing source data makes physical reconstruction underdetermined;
choose and declare a plausible model, then test the model's own output.
