# Twenty physics-led animation directions

Design brief: 20 distinct sequences, each targeting 30 seconds, with several connected events and a legible ending. These are direction scripts for discussion and later implementation. No scene has been generated, simulated, validated or approved by this document. The prior rejected hero is not reused as a physical reference.

## Rules shared by all twenty

- Build a continuous, supported space. Floors, rails, shafts, pipe walls, hinges and catches are real geometry with collision behavior. Materials and masses explain response; objects retain their shape and scale.
- Motion follows gravity, contact, friction and declared constraints. Show the energy source for powered action: a motor, elevated counterweight, wound spring, reservoir or circuit. A motor has a finite torque budget and can slow or stall under load.
- Allow appropriate recoil, spin, secondary impacts and settling. A heavy supported target can produce little visible recoil; the script must make that support and mass relationship credible. Do not impose bounce merely for decoration.
- A split releases components that were already present. A transformation uses visible hinges, latches or assemblies. Light is an appearance/signal effect powered by a declared circuit; it does not create matter or launch bodies.
- Pipes use concave interior walls or compound colliders, never a solid convex hull enclosing the bore. Rims, branches and entrances participate in contact.
- Keep near misses visibly clear of solid surfaces and causally distinct from hits. Do not start a reaction before contact, interpolate bodies through obstructions, shrink them through apertures, freeze a moving body or hide corrections behind edits.
- The six time windows below are provisional editorial targets. Tune distances, slopes, masses, friction, release conditions and actuator schedules, then resimulate. Do not retime baked physics or force body coordinates to hit the beat times. A mechanism may wait at a real latch; a freely moving body may not pause for the camera.
- Prefer a continuous three-quarter view with visible contact shadows and restrained materials. A cut may clarify a mechanism but cannot conceal a discontinuity. Use a fixed overview and side view during physical review. Preserve enough screen space and contrast to read every important contact.
- The last seconds contain a consequence and natural dissipation: a catch closes, a bowl rocks down, a beam settles or a flag is raised. Arrival does not erase velocity.
- Music suggestions below concern persistent object motifs, articulation, density and scene-timed Foley. Mechanical scenery can remain silent. A near miss can withhold resolution; it does not trigger an impact. Key changes retain the separate deliberate-control policy.

The existing production backend does not yet certify all these mechanisms. Coupled rigid bodies, motors, hinges and containment require fresh physical controls. Direction 09 additionally needs fluid/buoyancy validation; 10 needs a documented electromagnetic force model; 17 needs granular contact validation. None inherits a physics pass from a concept description. Existing penetration, support, causality, scale, energy/response, convergence and fresh-replay checks remain binding.

## 01 — The Hexagon Courier

**Direction:** Adapt the user's example into a copper-and-basalt delivery machine. A translucent spherical capsule contains three visibly distinct smaller balls. A triangular wedge ramp, powered hexagonal paddle wheel, pipe and cave form one readable descending route.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A retaining pin retracts. The capsule starts rolling down the triangular ramp, visibly accelerating and spinning. |
| 4–9 s | It reaches a hexagonal rotor with one outward board at each vertex. One approaching board strikes it obliquely; the motor supplies the added energy and the rotor takes the reaction load. |
| 9–14 s | The capsule travels along a ballistic arc into a flared pipe entrance, brushes the rim if the validated trajectory does so, and rolls through a transparent elbow. |
| 14–19 s | It meets a glowing padded stop in a cradle. Compression trips a latch; captive shell petals open, exposing the three balls that were present from the beginning. |
| 19–25 s | The balls leave through three descending channels: a curved ramp, two shallow steps and a banked bend. Their routes remain visible and separate. |
| 25–30 s | All three enter different sockets in the cave. Their weight depresses switches; a battery-powered checkpoint light comes on as the balls settle. |

**Mechanics:** Gravity plus a visible motor; the luminous stop is a switch, not a source of propulsion. The capsule shell remains in the cradle. No duplication or shrinking.

**Camera / score:** Follow the capsule until it opens, then widen to retain all three routes. One motif separates into three related instrumental voices.

## 02 — The Balance Market

**Direction:** A miniature brass market trades motion between three balance beams. Unequal arms and clearly different weights make leverage readable.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A bronze sphere rolls from an elevated shelf into a shallow cup on a balance beam. |
| 4–9 s | Its weight lowers the long arm; the opposite arm lifts a latched gate through a visible linkage. |
| 9–14 s | A waiting wooden cylinder is released onto a second ramp and rolls beneath the raised gate. |
| 14–19 s | The cylinder enters a second tipping cradle, which releases a previously elevated counterweight onto a third beam's cup. |
| 19–25 s | That beam lowers a small bridge. Reaching the bridge stop pulls a cable that unlatches the first cup's retaining lip; the original sphere rolls out and crosses. |
| 25–30 s | Sphere and cylinder arrive in adjacent cushioned trays. The beams make diminishing oscillations and stop against padded limits. |

**Mechanics:** Gravitational potential and lever ratios power the sequence. Every load stays supported; the cylinder does not move until its gate clears.

**Camera / score:** A side-biased overview reveals the arm lengths. Three short call-and-response phrases follow the successive transfers.

## 03 — The Pendulum Switchyard

**Direction:** A ball navigates a yard of slowly swinging pendulums, with one clean hit and one unmistakable near miss.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A raised pendulum is released while a small ball begins descending a separate ramp. |
| 4–9 s | The pendulum strikes a sliding latch, opening the ball's first gate and losing speed visibly. |
| 9–14 s | The ball passes through the pendulum's returning sweep with positive clearance; nothing reacts to this near miss. |
| 14–19 s | A banked curve delivers the ball to a second, lighter pendulum, which it genuinely contacts and sets swinging. |
| 19–25 s | The second pendulum pushes a hinged bridge into its receiving stop. The ball continues down a lower return lane and crosses it. |
| 25–30 s | The ball settles into an alcove while both pendulums retain small, decaying swings. |

**Mechanics:** Gravity powers the released pendulum and descending ball. Tune the route to the measured swing; never command the ball to dodge.

**Camera / score:** Keep the near miss in profile with the gap visible. Build a phrase toward it, withhold the accent, then articulate the real second contact.

## 04 — The Clockwork Flower

**Direction:** A porcelain-and-brass flower moves a seed through petal-shaped cups. Its apparent blooming is an exposed mechanical assembly.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A seed-shaped rigid capsule enters a pocket on a slowly turning screw elevator. |
| 4–9 s | The visible motor lifts it to a crown of six hinged petal cups. |
| 9–14 s | A cam tilts the first cup, allowing the seed to roll into the second; the first cup returns after the seed clears. |
| 14–19 s | Two more cups pass it around the crown, each with a real lip, hinge and mechanical stop. |
| 19–25 s | The final cup tips it into a descending spiral stem with an open viewing side. |
| 25–30 s | The seed reaches a rocking bowl at the flower's base; its weight operates a linkage that closes the petals slowly. |

**Mechanics:** A geared motor and cams drive the flower. The seed remains dynamic. Petals rotate as rigid parts and never stretch or morph.

**Camera / score:** A gentle orbit reveals the elevator and crown without hiding handoffs. Repeated motif notes change articulation with each different cup contact.

## 05 — The Domino Drawbridge

**Direction:** Dominoes build a route instead of merely falling in a row. A descending marble depends on two bridges being opened by the cascade.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A released pendulum tips the first domino; a marble waits behind a separate mechanical gate. |
| 4–9 s | The first domino chain turns a corner and knocks a bridge latch free. The bridge falls onto two visible supports. |
| 9–14 s | The bridge's landing depresses a lever that releases the marble onto the completed route. |
| 14–19 s | The marble hits a low paddle, starting a second, shorter domino chain alongside its descending track. |
| 19–25 s | That chain releases a second bridge before the marble reaches the gap. A rounded catch prevents the bridge from rebounding into the route. |
| 25–30 s | The marble crosses and settles in a terminal bowl; the last domino raises a small checkpoint flag through a lever. |

**Mechanics:** Elevated dominoes and bridges supply potential energy. Unstruck dominoes remain stable. Bridge timing comes from physical travel, not synchronized object keyframes.

**Camera / score:** Keep both the waiting marble and active chain visible. Domino pulses build rhythmic density, then leave space for the final rolling phrase.

## 06 — Three Ways Down

**Direction:** A solid sphere, solid cylinder and thin hoop leave the same height on separate parallel routes. Their different rotational inertia becomes the story.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | One crossbar releases all three from rest. Surface stripes make their rotation visible. |
| 4–9 s | They accelerate down equal initial slopes; the sphere, cylinder and hoop develop different speeds under rolling conditions. |
| 9–14 s | The sphere takes a longer banked detour, the cylinder crosses shallow corrugations, and the hoop passes through a broad guided bend. |
| 14–19 s | The lanes cross at different elevations, creating apparent convergence with clearly visible structural clearance. |
| 19–25 s | Each body depresses its own latch pedal and enters a separate cushioned dock. Arrival order is retained from the simulation. |
| 25–30 s | Only after all three pedals are down does a mechanical interlock release the shared finish flag. |

**Mechanics:** Use friction sufficient for the intended initial rolling. Different arrival times follow shape and route, not an invented rule that heavier objects fall faster. Guide the hoop without locking its motion to a path.

**Camera / score:** A wide side view makes the initial comparison fair. Three distinct motifs separate and later form a chord at the shared finish.

## 07 — The Recoil Post Office

**Direction:** A rolling launch cart sends a parcel across a tiny sorting hall. The camera makes the launch reaction as important as the projectile.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | Show a compressed spring between the ball and a wheeled launcher cart. A latch releases it. |
| 4–9 s | The ball accelerates forward while the cart recoils backward along its rail and compresses a rear buffer. |
| 9–14 s | The ball enters a broad banked receiver and redirects toward a hinged sorting paddle. |
| 14–19 s | Contact swings the paddle, sending the ball down the left chute and lifting a small destination flag. |
| 19–25 s | The chute contains a compliant bumper that produces one visible, lower-energy rebound before a descending return bend. |
| 25–30 s | The ball rolls into a padded parcel drawer, which slides slightly under its momentum and settles. |

**Mechanics:** The visible spring supplies launch energy. Cart mass, rail friction and buffer compression explain the recoil; it is not a decorative animation.

**Camera / score:** Hold the launcher in frame through the reaction, then track the ball. Pair the forward phrase with a short lower-register response from the cart contact.

## 08 — The Spiral Observatory

**Direction:** A glowing bead descends a miniature astronomical instrument of nested tracks, giving depth and repeated near encounters without contact fakery.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | Release the bead onto the outer rim of a sloping spiral track. |
| 4–9 s | It gains speed, banking through a broad turn above a stationary central sphere. |
| 9–14 s | A crossover carries it close to an earlier section of track, with vertical clearance plainly visible. |
| 14–19 s | It exits through a short gravity drop into a lower spiral turning in the opposite direction. |
| 19–25 s | A shallow bowl sends it around an off-centre drain; a textured lining and rolling resistance reduce its orbit before it enters the opening. |
| 25–30 s | A visible chute leads to a soft-lined central nest, where the bead settles and closes a contact-powered lamp circuit. |

**Mechanics:** Gravity and surface forces explain every turn. The central sphere is not an unexplained attractor. The bead's light does not affect its trajectory.

**Camera / score:** A restrained elevated camera exposes the crossover. Use long rolling phrases interrupted by the short drop and final settling notes.

## 09 — The Water Lock

**Direction:** A cork capsule travels through a small transparent canal lock and beaches inside a stone grotto.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A descending counterweight opens a valve from a visibly elevated water reservoir. |
| 4–9 s | Water raises a captive float, whose linkage opens the capsule's retaining gate. |
| 9–14 s | The capsule floats into the lock chamber, drifting and rotating with the actual flow. |
| 14–19 s | Its contact with a bumper releases the outlet valve; the water level starts falling and the lower gate becomes passable. |
| 19–25 s | The capsule follows the draining current through a curved channel beneath a low stone arch. |
| 25–30 s | It reaches a shallow gravel shelf inside the grotto, gently grounds and rocks to rest as the finite reservoir empties. |

**Mechanics:** Reservoir height supplies energy; buoyancy, displaced volume, fluid forces and wall contact must be simulated. No body is keyframed to follow a decorative water surface.

**Camera / score:** Keep water levels and the float linkage visible. Use a sustained flowing texture with sparse capsule-contact notes. Advanced fluid direction.

## 10 — The Magnetic Switchyard

**Direction:** A steel bead moves through a glass-sided rail channel whose coils light only while powered. A wooden companion reveals which effects are magnetic.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | The steel bead rolls down a starter slope toward an illuminated coil. A visible battery and circuit establish the power source. |
| 4–9 s | Measured coil attraction accelerates it through the first section; current switches off as it passes the sensor. |
| 9–14 s | A second coil biases it into a branching rail while an adjacent wooden ball continues under gravity along a straight route. |
| 14–19 s | The steel bead contacts a mechanical switch lever that opens a gate for the wooden ball. |
| 19–25 s | Both descend unpowered return channels. Rough lining removes speed before their receiving pockets. |
| 25–30 s | They settle side by side; the final occupied-pocket switches turn the coil lights off. |

**Mechanics:** Use a documented force field with visible power timing, not attraction-shaped position curves. The wooden ball remains nonmagnetic. Electrical work enters the energy account.

**Camera / score:** Keep circuit state and bead position readable together. Contrast the steel bead's sharp articulation with the wooden ball's softer attacks. Advanced field-model direction.

## 11 — The Cable-Car Trade

**Direction:** Two counterbalanced gondolas exchange a stone and a brass sphere across a small ravine.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A stone slides down a short hopper into one gondola; the opposite car carries a lighter brass sphere. |
| 4–9 s | The imbalance lowers the stone car and raises the brass car through a visible pulley and taut cable. |
| 9–14 s | The cars engage station catches. A fixed cam at the upper station tips the brass car's captive tray, releasing the sphere onto a supported track. |
| 14–19 s | The sphere follows a zigzag route and presses a lever that unlatches the stone car's unloading tray. |
| 19–25 s | The stone slides into its receiving bin. A small spring take-up keeps the unloaded cable seated as the cars settle against station buffers. |
| 25–30 s | The brass sphere arrives beside the stone through a lower tunnel and comes to rest in its own socket. |

**Mechanics:** The descending heavier load supplies the lift energy. Cable tension, pulley rotation, changing loads and buffers must remain consistent.

**Camera / score:** Use the ravine's side elevation to show both cars. Rising and falling lines exchange the lead without automatic harmonic modulation.

## 12 — The Newton Relay

**Direction:** A hanging-ball cradle passes an impulse into a separate marble route, making a legitimate abrupt stop understandable through motion elsewhere.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A lifted steel pendulum ball is released toward four aligned suspended balls. |
| 4–9 s | Contact transfers motion through the row; the outgoing end ball swings toward a release lever. |
| 9–14 s | The end ball strikes the lever, freeing a small ceramic marble from an elevated gate. The cradle continues its diminishing exchanges. |
| 14–19 s | The marble rolls through an S-bend and narrowly passes a returning cradle ball in a separate, visibly clear lane. |
| 19–25 s | It drops into a pivoted spoon, tipping it and rolling onward into a lower channel. |
| 25–30 s | The marble settles in a felt-lined recess while the cradle still shows small, physically decaying motion. |

**Mechanics:** Contact response follows measured restitution and mass. The first ball may slow sharply because momentum is transferred; the receiver's movement must establish that cause.

**Camera / score:** A fixed lateral camera shows the whole transfer. Pass a short motif between voices, then let the ceramic marble take a lighter variation.

## 13 — The Pinwheel Quarry

**Direction:** A descending stone powers three free-spinning paddle wheels, each changing its route and surrendering some of its energy.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A rounded stone leaves a retaining gate on a steep quarry ramp. |
| 4–9 s | It hits the first wheel's paddle, starting the axle turning and deflecting into a lower chute. |
| 9–14 s | A second wheel has a broader receiving pocket; it turns under the stone's weight and drops the stone at a lower angle. |
| 14–19 s | The stone passes through the spokes of a third wheel with a clear gap, missing its paddle and leaving that wheel stationary. |
| 19–25 s | It reaches a rough switchback, makes two smaller contacts and loses speed. The first wheels coast and slow under bearing friction. |
| 25–30 s | The stone arrives in a cave bowl and settles against two already stationary pebbles. |

**Mechanics:** No motors: all wheel motion comes from the stone's descending energy. A missed paddle never begins turning. Rotor inertia must affect the stone's outgoing speed.

**Camera / score:** Keep the stationary third wheel visible after the miss. Use two real impact accents, a withheld third accent, then a quieter landing.

## 14 — The Marble Decision Tree

**Direction:** Three differently coloured marbles negotiate a transparent branching machine whose gates remember earlier contacts mechanically.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A mechanical escapement releases the three marbles sequentially from a short queue. |
| 4–9 s | The first marble tips a bistable flap and takes the left chute; the flap remains against its new stop. |
| 9–14 s | The second reaches the changed flap and takes the right chute, then tips another gate downstream. |
| 14–19 s | The third follows the now-altered route and passes beneath the first marble's elevated crossing. |
| 19–25 s | The three lanes introduce different textures: a smooth bowl, shallow steps and a long curve, producing distinct approach rhythms. |
| 25–30 s | Each marble reaches a different cave pocket. Their occupied catches complete a visible three-part mechanical indicator. |

**Mechanics:** Gate state follows actual impacts, over-centre springs and hard stops. Route choices are consequences of geometry; they are not reassigned after a simulation misses its intended branch.

**Camera / score:** A front three-quarter view reads like a branching diagram. Maintain one motif per marble as their phrases interleave.

## 15 — The Folding Canyon

**Direction:** Flat triangular panels unfold into a usable route through a canyon. The transformations are rigid rotations around visible hinges.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A falling counterweight begins drawing a cable through the first panel's pulley. |
| 4–9 s | Two hinged panels open in sequence and rest on structural ledges, forming a descending V-shaped trough. |
| 9–14 s | The second panel's completed motion withdraws a pin holding a faceted rolling body. |
| 14–19 s | The body tumbles and rolls through the trough; a real contact releases a third panel, which drops onto a receiving stop. |
| 19–25 s | That panel completes a bridge into a rock corridor. The body crosses with visible rocking caused by its facets. |
| 25–30 s | It enters a broad stone socket, rocks through decreasing angles and rests. The counterweight stops on a padded support. |

**Mechanics:** Panel dimensions never change. Hinges, cables and stops carry loads. Do not let a folding panel sweep through the rolling body or existing structure.

**Camera / score:** Show panel thickness and hinge axes. Use sustained panel-motion phrasing and uneven, sparse attacks for the faceted body.

## 16 — The Flywheel Ferry

**Direction:** A powered horizontal turntable carries a small puck toward a release gate, then hands it to a stationary descending route.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A puck slides from a short feed ramp into a shallow pocket near the turntable rim. |
| 4–9 s | A belt-driven motor accelerates the table gradually. The pocket wall supplies the puck's centripetal force. |
| 9–14 s | A fixed cam retracts the pocket's outer gate at the transfer station. The puck leaves with the velocity it actually has at release. |
| 14–19 s | A wide receiver catches the tangential departure and guides it into a banked descending channel. |
| 19–25 s | The puck contacts a sliding shutter, revealing a final tunnel before continuing through the opening. |
| 25–30 s | A roughened run-out and spring buffer bring it to rest in the dock while the motor ramps the empty turntable down. |

**Mechanics:** The motor supplies rotational energy; release does not create a radial launch impulse. The receiver is positioned from the validated departure trajectory.

**Camera / score:** Begin near overhead so the tangent is obvious, then lower gently. A circular rhythmic figure releases into a straighter phrase.

## 17 — The Hourglass Gate

**Direction:** A controlled stream of grains opens a route for one larger object, turning gradual accumulation into a clear threshold event.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A shutter opens beneath a finite hopper of small grains. A large ball waits at a latched ramp entrance. |
| 4–9 s | Grains fall through a narrow channel into a balance pan, colliding, piling and settling rather than becoming a liquid surface. |
| 9–14 s | The increasing load overcomes the opposite counterweight and lowers the pan, pulling the ball's latch free. |
| 14–19 s | The ball rolls onto a separate solid track beside the grain stream and nudges a flap that closes the hopper shutter. |
| 19–25 s | It descends two banked turns while the last released grains land in the pan. |
| 25–30 s | The ball reaches a cave cradle; the balance and remaining grains settle, leaving the completed mound visible. |

**Mechanics:** Use a finite, bounded grain count with validated size, mass, friction and contact response. The gate moves because of measured load, not an arbitrary timer.

**Camera / score:** Frame the pan, waiting ball and latch together during the threshold. A granular texture gives way to one clear rolling motif. Advanced granular direction.

## 18 — The Billiard Greenhouse

**Direction:** A ceramic puck ricochets through a small conservatory of hinged glass fins and wooden rails, opening a final door through a causal bank shot.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A visible spring plunger launches the puck across a shallow inclined table. |
| 4–9 s | It banks off a fixed wooden rail, rotating slightly when the contact is off-centre. |
| 9–14 s | A grazing contact swings a hinged glass fin; the puck loses speed and changes direction while the fin continues moving. |
| 14–19 s | The puck threads a gap between two stationary fins without touching them, then strikes a low release peg. |
| 19–25 s | The peg releases a counterweighted greenhouse door. The puck follows a descending groove toward the opening. |
| 25–30 s | It enters a planting alcove and settles against a compliant stop; the struck fin's motion decays in the background. |

**Mechanics:** Rails and fins have finite thickness. The hinged fin absorbs angular momentum. Glancing and missed contacts must remain distinct in both animation and sound.

**Camera / score:** Keep reflected highlights restrained so glass edges remain visible. Use clipped bank-shot accents and a lighter articulation for the glancing fin contact.

## 19 — The Wheel Within the Wheel

**Direction:** A small ball rides inside a large open hoop as both descend, then escapes through a real hatch and completes the route alone.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | A retaining bar releases a thick-rimmed hoop on a gentle ramp. A small ball is visibly enclosed inside it. |
| 4–9 s | The hoop rolls while the inner ball repeatedly shifts around its lower interior under gravity and contact. |
| 9–14 s | Guide rails keep the hoop on the ramp without fixing its speed. The inner ball's contact pattern changes as the slope steepens. |
| 14–19 s | The hoop reaches a cushioned docking fork. A fixed cam opens a hinged section of its rim only after it is aligned and supported. |
| 19–25 s | The inner ball rolls through that opening into a narrow chute, while the hoop rocks within the dock and loses energy. |
| 25–30 s | The ball reaches a circular niche below; both bodies finish supported and still. |

**Mechanics:** Use a hollow ring collider and a separate hinged hatch. The ball exists inside the hoop throughout. Docking, opening and release need coupled contact and hinge validation.

**Camera / score:** An oblique side view keeps the inner ball visible. Two nested rhythmic patterns separate when the smaller body exits.

## 20 — The Three Keys to the Cave

**Direction:** A sphere, cylinder and hinged weight each complete a different task. The cave opens only when all three physical conditions have been met.

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–4 s | One release bar starts a sphere down a ramp, a cylinder down a second track, and an elevated pendulum weight swinging. |
| 4–9 s | The pendulum strikes a lever that places a bridge across the sphere's route, then swings back with reduced energy. |
| 9–14 s | The cylinder reaches a treadle and depresses it, withdrawing the first cave-door bolt. It stays cradled on the treadle. |
| 14–19 s | The sphere crosses the supported bridge, makes a short controlled drop and lands in a bowl that withdraws the second bolt and arms a previously retracted pendulum catch. |
| 19–25 s | On its next qualifying swing, the pendulum engages the armed catch and releases the final latch. A visible counterweight raises the cave gate into an open-position catch. |
| 25–30 s | The gate's linkage tips the two waiting cradles toward the cave. Sphere and cylinder roll inside and settle in separate berths; the pendulum remains captured at its padded catch. |

**Mechanics:** Separate bolts form a real mechanical AND condition. The gate's counterweight supplies its lifting energy. Waiting objects are held by cradles, not paused in space.

**Camera / score:** A wide composition preserves all three causes, then eases toward the entrance. Independent motifs converge into a final shared phrase after the last real latch release.
